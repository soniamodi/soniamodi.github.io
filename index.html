LEGO

Weight-driven pendulum clock

This is my favorite LEGO project I've done so far: a pendulum clock built from 421 LEGO parts. The hard part was fitting the gear train, escapement, and winding differential together using only parts that actually exist. The differential lets the weight be rewound by hand while the gear chain keeps the clock running.

The pendulum needs a one-second period, which works out to a length of about 24.8 cm.

Game Development

Roblox · Luau

MartMart, a multiplayer market simulator

MartMart is a multiplayer store-management game I built in Roblox Studio. I was the sole-developer and programmed everything, created all assets, and animated all motions.

The core loop: order inventory on the in-game laptop, drive to the warehouse, load and place products, stock shelves, open the store, serve customers, deposit the earnings at the ATM, and repeat.

Players make parties in a lobby, pick a save slot, and the party leader can kick members. I made the interface art and animation for the lobby myself.

https://github.com/soniamodi/MARKET-SIM-ROBLOX-REPO.git

MartMart · Economy

The laptop

The in-game laptop has a home page with save slots and stats, plus tabs for four supply stores and the bank. Purchases deduct an item's price times its quantity, and selling returns 75% of the price. The server records every transaction with a date and updates every player's laptop.

Each item has its own stock probability and maximum. Every 5 minutes, stock regenerates from a seed based on the current time interval, so separate game servers produce identical stock without ever communicating.

MartMart · Blender

The town and vehicles

The town connects the four places in the gameplay loop: the warehouse, bank, car dealership, and market build area. Players drive to the warehouse and have to cross a street where cars can actually hit them.

I modeled the player car in Blender and scripted it myself, including a speedometer on the dashboard.

MartMart · Gameplay

Build mode, customers, and checkout

Build mode lets players drag objects, rotate them along each axis, and destroy placed objects. A placed object has to intersect another structure to stay in place, otherwise physics takes over when the player lets go.

While the store is open, a customer has a 50% chance to spawn every five seconds, wearing a random player's avatar. Each one generates a shopping list of up to five items and finds them with Roblox pathfinding, then queues at the register. Earnings are collected as a money bag after closing and deposited at the ATM.

Research

Math

Strong resilience of dominating sets

Pham Discrete Mathematics Research Group, Alabama A&M

I cold-emailed a graph theory professor and ended up independently developing proofs and combinatorial formulas for the strong resilience of a fixed dominating set under random edge failures. I implemented the computational methods in Python and co-first-authored a paper introducing the new graph resilience metric and analyzing its properties. Dr. Andrew Pham and I co-first authored a paper, the abstract of which is attached.

Abstract

Given a simple finite graph G = (V(G), E(G)), a vertex subset D ⊆ V(G) is a dominating set of G if for each v ∈ V(G) − D, there exists a vertex u ∈ N(v) ∩ D. When an edge subset B ⊆ E(G) is deleted, the set D may no longer be a dominating set of the resulting graph G − B; however, there may exist another dominating set of G − B with equal cardinality. In real-world practical settings, such a reconfiguration of vertices may not be feasible. It is therefore of interest to study the properties of a fixed dominating set. We introduce a new parameter, the strong resilience RG(D, t) of a fixed dominating set D under the deletion of t edges. We investigate fundamental properties of the strong resilience, methods for its computation, and potential applications.

Hydropower

Wave energy arrays around a retired platform

Structural Performance & Fluid-Structure Interaction (SP-I) Lab, University of Houston

Inspired by debate and the struggles of my hometown Houston, I cold-emailed Dr. Dimitrios Kalliontzis. Working with Dr. Kalliontzis, I mapped the platform's geometry, built buoy and pile meshes, and developed Capytaine boundary-element simulations of eight-buoy arrays. I generated 2,287 structured and Latin hypercube layouts, calculated their q-factors, ran statistical and SHAP analyses, and co-first-authored a paper, the abstract of which is attached.

Abstract

The decommissioning of aging Gulf of America oil and gas platforms threatens the ecological and economic benefits they provide, motivating alternative end-of-life pathways such as hosting wave energy converters. This study investigates how arrays of wave energy converters (WECs) interact hydrodynamically when placed around an existing retired platform, namely the South Pass 83 (SP-83A). Eight heaving point absorbers were modeled with the open-source boundary element solver Capytaine under linear potential-flow theory and optimal control, using a monochromatic 6s wave from a 315° heading. Layout quality was measured with the q-factor, i.e., the ratio of array power to that of the same number of isolated devices. We evaluated 1,287 structured zone-based layouts and 1,000 Latin hypercube layouts, each with and without the platform's ten piles. Unstructured layouts performed better: mean q was 0.975 versus 0.869, 40.8% versus 5.3% of layouts exceeded q=1, with the highest q being 1.391 versus 1.110. High-q arrays spread buoys across the wave front and avoided close spacing, with the best layouts keeping devices roughly half a wavelength apart. The piles raised mean q by 0.0150 (structured) and 0.0066 (Latin hypercube), under 2% of the pile-free value, and largely preserved layout rankings (Pearson r=0.973). Individual layouts shifted by up to about ±0.08, but the piles did however reorder similarly ranked layouts. Buoy placement matters more than a platform's presence, which acts as a second-order effect. Results are limited to one sea state and idealized assumptions.

Astrophysics

Summer Science Program

Astrophysics at Colby College, June 22 to July 24, 2026

SSP was a five-week, college-level research program where my team did every step of asteroid orbit determination ourselves: pointing the telescope, taking images of the near-Earth asteroid 1943 Anteros, converting those images into positions on the sky, and writing the code that turns those positions into an orbit.

We used the Method of Gauss to calculate the asteroid's six orbital elements, then ran 100,000 Monte Carlo samples to put uncertainties on them. Most of the uncertainty traces back to the astrometry step, since the telescope's resolution limits how precisely we can measure position. With the orbit in hand, we could generate an ephemeris to guide future observations of the asteroid, and our observations were submitted to the Minor Planet Center.

The program doesn't allow the full write-up to be shared, so this is just an overview. We also took a color image of Stephens Quintet.

Space

Astronomy

Discovering a variable star

2MASS J14400154+4431272

This started as a side project just for fun. I wrote a Python pipeline that pulls Gaia and ZTF survey data, analyzes stellar light curves, and extracts variability features for thousands of stars.

An Isolation Forest model flagged the stars that behaved unusually, and I used Lomb-Scargle periodograms and Box Least Squares to study their periodic behavior. After cross-referencing existing catalogs, I found a star that had never been classified: a new Gamma Doradus (GDOR) variable that pulsates with a period of about 0.36 days.

I submitted it to the AAVSO International Variable Star Index, the submission was approved, and I'm officially credited as its discoverer.
